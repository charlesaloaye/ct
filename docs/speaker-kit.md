## Talk 1: Rescuing the codebase nobody wants to touch

**Format:** talk (25–30 min) or workshop (90 min)
**Level:** intermediate
**Audience:** developers, tech leads, founders with a stalled product

**Abstract:**
Every team inherits it eventually: half-finished code, a missing developer and a launch date that won't move. This talk is a practical routine for the first week of a rescue: how to read a codebase fast, decide what's salvageable, stabilise before adding features, and report honestly to the people paying for it. Examples come from real rescue projects on Laravel and JavaScript stacks.

**Attendees leave with:**
- a first-week rescue checklist;
- a way to estimate a rescue without guessing;
- the warning signs that mean "rewrite this part".

## Talk 2: Building for students on a data budget

**Format:** talk (25–30 min)
**Level:** all levels
**Audience:** developers, product people, edtech founders

**Abstract:**
Built Different runs a family of student products: JAMB Advisor, UniAdvisor, DelsuTools, StudyAbroad.ng and ScholarshipHQ.ng. This talk covers what changes when your users are Nigerian students on mobile data. It looks at choosing small focused tools over one big app, keeping pages fast and light, making information trustworthy, and growing through search rather than paid ads.

**Attendees leave with:**
- design and performance decisions that matter for low-bandwidth users;
- a model for running several small products as one studio.

## Talk 3: Letting an AI agent call your tools safely

**Format:** talk (25–30 min) or lightning talk (10 min)
**Level:** intermediate
**Audience:** developers working with AI agents, automation and APIs

**Abstract:**
When an AI agent can call your API on its own, every unclear input and silent failure becomes a problem. Using an Apify Instagram analyzer actor as the case study (written up on Apify's DEV page), this talk shows the guardrails that made it safe for agents to call, and what broke first when it was wired into Slack through an MCP connector.

**Attendees leave with:**
- a checklist for making a tool agent-safe: clear input schemas, limits, explicit errors and idempotency;
- lessons from a real MCP integration.
